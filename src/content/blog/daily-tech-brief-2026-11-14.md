## The FAA grounded the whole country for 4.5 hours

At 3:54 p.m. ET Friday, the FAA ordered a nationwide ground stop for all departing flights, "due to extraordinary circumstances and for reasons of safety," with no other explanation. It lifted the stop at 8:15 p.m. By then more than 7,000 flights were canceled and airports were full of stranded passengers. It was the first full ground stop since January 2023.

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy's explanation: an outdated air traffic control system where routers "got overloaded with data," causing "instability for our radar and our communications in our centers, in our TRACONS throughout the country."

One wrinkle. Futurism reports DHS is looking at whether the FAA's SMART tool, an AI air traffic system expanded across the country days earlier, was involved. The official line is router overload. The question DHS is asking is the right one: when the new AI layer sits on top of 40-year-old infrastructure, which one failed?

At the same time, Amtrak's booking system broke too. One bad night to be a national network.

[Source](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/us-news/faa-orders-nationwide-ground-stop-departing-flights-reasons-safety-rcna609247) and [DHS angle](https://futurism.com/)

## OpenAI gave its agents a credit card

OpenAI launched ChatGPT Wallet on Friday. Its Dots agents can now buy things for you. You add a card once, your dot finds the item, and you approve each purchase with Face ID or a fingerprint.

The part that matters: the agent never sees your card. Amex, Mastercard, or Visa issues a payment token, a scoped card number, security code, and expiration good for that one transaction. OpenAI takes no cut to start. Pro subscribers in the US first.

Context worth knowing: ChatGPT has 1.2 billion weekly users and about 100 million paying users. And not everyone wants the agents. United Airlines said Wednesday it will block agents from booking tickets. Amazon blocks them from its platform entirely.

The payment networks just legitimized agent commerce. The merchants are split.

[Source](https://openai.com/index/introducing-chatgpt-wallet/) and [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/13/openai-chatgpt-wallet-dot-purchases.html)

## Every Shopify store now speaks agent

Sierra released Personal Agent Protocol v1.0, called Poppy. A business publishes one file at /.well-known/poppy.json that tells your personal agent how to interact with it: how you sign in, what the agent is allowed to do, how sessions work. No more agents pretending to be you in a checkout flow built for humans.

Adoption on day one is the story. Poppy is live in Meta's Muse, auto-published for every Shopify merchant in 175-plus countries, and backed by 100-plus design partners: OpenAI, Stripe, Visa, Mastercard, PayPal, Walmart, Target, Bank of America, United Airlines, and more.

Agents needed a standard handshake with businesses. This is the first serious attempt, and it launched with real distribution.

[Source](https://sierra.ai/blog/poppy)

## Tesla opened Cybercab orders: $29,990, plus $299 a month, forever

Tesla invited select customers Friday to order the Cybercab for personal use. $29,990 with a $1,000 non-refundable deposit. Two seats, no steering wheel, no pedals. Deliveries estimated from December 2026.

The catches: a mandatory $299-per-month Autonomy Subscription, it only works inside supported Robotaxi service areas, and you cannot use it commercially.

That $299 a month is $3,588 a year. Over five years the subscription costs more than half the car. Tesla is not selling you a car. It is renting you a driver and throwing in a car.

[Source](https://electrek.co/2026/11/13/tesla-cybercab-personal-use-orders-299-subscription-geofence/)

## Nvidia got accused of manufacturing its own shortage

A TechPowerUp report topped Hacker News Friday: Nvidia allegedly funded AI startups that then spent the money on Nvidia GPUs, engineering the scarcity and the demand loop at the same time. Alleged, not adjudicated. But it matches what everyone in procurement has felt for two years.

Separately, the FT reports that despite 20-plus years selling into China, Jensen Huang never built direct access to the policymakers now driving Beijing's chip self-sufficiency push, right as China limits H200 imports and pushes homegrown silicon.

The pickaxe seller had a historic run. Both stories this week are about what happens when the gold rush matures.

[HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50493251) and [report](https://www.techpowerup.com/354814/nvidia-allegedly-engineered-scarcity-by-funding-ai-startups-to-buy-its-gpus)

## ggml hit 1.0

The tensor library under llama.cpp and whisper.cpp shipped v1.0.0 Friday. It prunes the deprecated public API and legacy DIAG_MASK ops, and adds configurable YaRN truncation. Local inference keeps growing up while the cloud guys argue about agents.

[Source](https://github.com/ggml-org/ggml/releases/tag/v1.0.0)

## The through-line: delegation

Every story today is about handing a job to software. Your agent gets your credit card. Your store talks to other people's agents. Your car has no wheel because you are not driving. Your flight stopped because a router drowned in data, and DHS wants to know if the new AI layer helped drown it.

Delegation works until the system hiccups. Then you find out real fast what you actually handed over. Keep the receipts, keep the kill switch, and read what your agent is allowed to do before you let it do anything.
